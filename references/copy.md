# Copy

## On the card

**Eyebrow** — where it happened. `brand` is the domain, `context` is the section:
`clawhub.ai` / `skills · trending`. Lower case. This is a source line, not a slogan.

**Hero** — the number and nothing else. `prefix` `#`, `value` `1`. The two tag lines
beside it read as one phrase top to bottom: `trending` / `on ClawHub`.

**Headline** — the name of the thing. Not a sentence, not a claim, no verb. The
numeral already made the claim.

**Subtitle** — one sentence, about 15 words, on what it does for the people using
it. Write the outcome, not the category.

> Persistent file-based planning that keeps AI coding agents on task across
> crashes, context loss and compaction.

not

> A markdown-based planning system for AI agents.

The first tells a stranger why 26.000 people starred it. The second is a shelf label.

**Stats** — three or four, chosen to answer the sceptic's question, which is always
some form of *is this real or did it just happen once*. Longevity, breadth and
adoption beat vanity. Prefer stars, forks, integrations, age. Skip anything that
needs a footnote.

**Author line** — name, role, city, and at most two links. Nobody types a third one.

## The caption

The image carries the claim, the caption carries the story. Four beats:

1. **The fact, dated.** One line. No throat-clearing.
2. **What it does, for whom.** One or two sentences, plain, no adjective stacking.
3. **One concrete detail** that is only true of this project: an unexpected
   integration, who contributed, what it was built for originally.
4. **One link.** One.

Optionally a fifth: what the person is open to next. Only if they said to include it.

### Worked example, X

> Planning with Files is trending #1 on ClawHub today.
>
> It keeps AI coding agents on task when the session dies: the plan, the findings
> and the progress live in markdown on disk, so a crash or a context reset costs
> you nothing.
>
> 26.612 stars, 2.219 forks, running on 60+ agents. Started in January.
>
> github.com/OthmanAdi/planning-with-files

### Worked example, LinkedIn

Same facts, one added line of context on why the problem exists, and a closing line
that is either a thank-you to contributors or a statement of what is next. LinkedIn
tolerates three short paragraphs; it does not tolerate a thread.

### Worked example, email

Subject: the fact, no more. `Planning with Files is #1 on ClawHub`. Banner image at
the top, two sentences under it, one link. Newsletters are read in a preview pane.

## Register

- State the fact and stop. The number is the excitement; the prose does not need to
  supply any.
- No dashes as pauses. Commas, colons, parentheses, or a new sentence.
- No "thrilled", "humbled", "incredible journey", "couldn't be prouder". A dated
  fact and a working link outperform all of them.
- No emoji rows. One, at most, and only if that is how the person already writes.
- Credit contributors by name if they agreed to be named, otherwise as a group.
- Write in the person's own language and register. If they write German posts, the
  caption is German; the card's labels can stay English if the platform is English.

## What not to claim

`references/verify.md` has the list. The short version: date every ranking, round
down, and never name a competitor you beat.
